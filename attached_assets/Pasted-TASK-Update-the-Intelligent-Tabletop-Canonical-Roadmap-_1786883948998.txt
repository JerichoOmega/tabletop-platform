OBJECTIVE

Update the repository's canonical project documentation so that ALL future Replit agents, coding agents, AI systems, and developers understand the intended long-term roadmap and architecture.

A new core design principle has been established:

THE TABLE IS FIXED. THE WORLD IS NOT.

Intelligent Tabletop should not treat the visible tabletop dimensions as the maximum size of the game world.

Instead:

The tabletop is a fixed-size viewport into a potentially much larger persistent world.

A dungeon, corridor, battlefield, city, cavern, forest, or other environment may be substantially larger than the visible tabletop.

The system should represent only the relevant portion of that world on the tabletop and move the viewport through the larger world as the player explores.

CORE DESIGN PRINCIPLE

Document this prominently:

"The Table Is Fixed. The World Is Not."

Explain:

- The visible tabletop has a fixed presentation size.
- The represented world may be arbitrarily larger.
- The tabletop acts as a viewport into world space.
- Characters and world objects retain persistent world coordinates.
- Moving the viewport must not change an object's actual world position.
- Large environments should not be artificially compressed merely to fit the tabletop.
- Small environments may fit entirely within the tabletop.
- Large environments may require viewport movement.
- Long environments such as corridors may stream/recycle world geometry as the party progresses.

WORLD REPRESENTATION MODEL

Document the intended conceptual architecture:

WORLD
↓
WORLD REGION
↓
WORLD COORDINATES
↓
VIEWPORT
↓
VISIBLE WORLD SECTION
↓
TABLETOP RENDERER

Clarify that this is a future architectural model, not yet an implemented system.

The existing rules engine should remain authoritative over game state and world coordinates.

The viewport should determine what portion of the world is presented, not redefine the world itself.

EXAMPLES

Document examples such as:

SMALL ROOM:
The entire room fits on the tabletop.

LARGE ROOM:
The room exceeds the tabletop dimensions and the viewport moves as the party explores.

LONG CORRIDOR:
The corridor may be much longer than the tabletop. As the party advances, previously viewed sections leave the viewport and new sections appear ahead.

MASSIVE BATTLEFIELD:
The battlefield may extend far beyond the visible tabletop while maintaining persistent world coordinates.

OPEN WORLD / LARGE ENVIRONMENT:
The tabletop represents the portion of the environment currently relevant to the party.

IMPORTANT UX PRINCIPLE

The player should not feel like they are playing on a tiny board with artificial boundaries.

The intended experience is:

"The table stays the same size, but the world comes to the table."

Avoid describing the system as merely "camera scrolling."

The desired experience is that the tabletop feels like a fixed physical game surface capable of representing a much larger world.

EXPLORATION VS TACTICAL COMBAT

Document the intended distinction:

EXPLORATION MODE:
- The viewport may move through the world.
- Large environments can continuously reveal new areas.
- Long corridors and roads can extend beyond the visible tabletop.
- The world may stream/recycle presentation geometry.
- The party can travel through spaces larger than the tabletop.

TACTICAL MODE:
- The relevant tactical area becomes stable.
- The tabletop should behave like a traditional tactical board.
- Movement, targeting, attacks, and abilities operate against a stable spatial presentation.
- The viewport should not unexpectedly drift while the player is making tactical decisions.

ENCOUNTER TRANSITION:

Exploration
↓
Encounter detected
↓
Relevant tactical area established
↓
Viewport stabilizes
↓
Combat
↓
Combat ends
↓
Exploration resumes

This is a planned behavior, not an implementation requirement for this documentation task.

PERSISTENCE REQUIREMENT

Document that future implementation must preserve world identity.

For example:

If an entity exists at world coordinate (142, 37), changing the viewport must not cause that entity to become a different entity or regenerate into an inconsistent state.

Returning to a previously explored location should represent the same world state unless game systems have intentionally changed it.

ARCHITECTURAL BOUNDARY

Explicitly state:

The World Scale / Viewport system must NOT replace the authoritative rules engine.

The conceptual separation should remain:

RULES ENGINE
- authoritative game state
- movement legality
- combat legality
- world/entity state
- authoritative coordinates

WORLD REPRESENTATION
- world regions
- viewport position
- visible world section
- streaming/chunk presentation

TABLETOP UI
- renders the currently visible world section
- presents interaction state
- collects player input

ASSET SYSTEM
- provides visual representations
- remains independent from gameplay legality

AI / INTENT SYSTEM
- reasons about/proposes actions
- must operate through authoritative validation
- should eventually be able to reason about world context without owning world state

FUTURE AI INTEGRATION

Document that this architecture is intentionally being established before expanding LLM-driven world generation or natural-language gameplay.

Eventually the AI DM may describe or generate environments much larger than the tabletop.

For example:

"The corridor continues another hundred feet before opening into a massive underground chamber."

The AI should describe the world/environment conceptually.

The world representation system should determine how that environment is represented through world coordinates, regions/chunks, viewport state, and tabletop rendering.

The AI should NOT directly manipulate the tabletop rendering layer.
